Key: arrow = holds the next role, or may use the data · grey = data

Access is a chain of roles; the app shows the whole chain.

  • Shows who can reach which data in Snowflake, and through which roles.
  • Snowflake: the company's cloud data warehouse.
  • A role is a named bundle of permissions; a grant gives one permission.
  • Depth = roles between person and data, the last one included.
  • Answers at any depth: access ten roles deep shows next to direct grants.
  • Only reads; never gives or removes a permission.
  • No app allowed? Five ready queries give the same answers in Snowflake.
  • Why: access is inherited role by role, hard to trace by hand.
  • My access: your own access, local version with your own sign-in only.
  • Who uses which screen: Not defined in source — confirm with owner.
  • Named owners or approvers of the tool: Not defined in source — confirm with owner.

Value

  • Full path for every answer, e.g. person > group > job role > data role > table.
  • 29 automatic checks, each with fix text for the change team.
  • "What if" shows who would lose access before anything is changed.
  • Sensitive data view: who reads labelled columns, and which are hidden (masked).
  • Activity tab on a table, a person or a role: who used which tables in the last 60 days, with which role and client, and how much of it outside business hours; query metadata only, never the query text. It follows the object: for a Streamlit app, who opened it and who saw data they cannot read themselves; for a database or schema, everything in it; for a warehouse, who ran queries on it and who can use it but did not.
  • SQL behind every page and card: a </> mark in each card's corner, the top-bar button or a right-click lists the read-only queries behind what the page shows, ready to copy and run in Snowflake with your own role; the app never runs them.

Limits

  • Snowflake updates its history with a lag of up to about 2 hours.
  • Only grants on that exact table count; ask again for its schema or database.
  • Future grants are not included; no masking or row rules.

Read-only promise

Does

  • Reads permissions, users, labels, hiding (masking) rules, query history.
  • May run the snapshot procedure that fills its own copy tables (shared-copy mode).
  • Keeps a local copy for a day.

Never

  • Runs GRANT, REVOKE, CREATE, ALTER or DROP.
  • Reads business data.
  • Stores passwords.
  • Applies a "what if" or a fix.

Key: left = allowed · right = never

Changes stay with the change team.

  • An automated test records every statement the app sends and allows reads only.
  • The only exception it allows: running the snapshot procedure.
  • Inside Snowflake it keeps nothing on disk, memory only.
  • CSV downloads cannot run spreadsheet formulas: a cell starting with = + - @ is marked as text.
  • The queries without the app also only read Snowflake's account history.

Costs and risks

Top 3 risks

  1. Answers can be a few hours old.
    • What: data as of the last read, plus up to ~2 h Snowflake delay; kept 24 h.
    • Impact: a just-removed permission may still show.
    • Fix: Read Snowflake again (direct), or Rebuild snapshot (shared copy).
    • Snowflake's own delay of up to ~2 h remains either way.
  2. Answers can be incomplete.
    • What: the reading role may lack rights.
    • Sensitive labels need Snowflake's Enterprise edition (a paid tier).
    • Impact: an audit answer may miss access.
    • Fix: the "Incomplete" box lists what is missing and the grant that fixes it.
  3. Some results are estimates.
    • What: hiding rules are read from their text, not tested.
    • "Read-only role can write" (check 17) is a guess.
    • Impact: a wrong finding gets acted on.
    • Fix: review before acting; unclear hiding rules show "unknown".

Decision needed

  • Choose how the team runs it: laptops (default) or inside Snowflake.
  • Choose the data source: read directly (nothing created) or a shared copy.
  • A shared copy needs a daily refresh you schedule; the repo has none.
  • Choose the reading role: wider read rights show the whole account, faster.
  • Choose whether auditors get the queries without the app.
  • Who approves these choices: Not defined in source — confirm with owner.
